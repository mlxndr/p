## Pre-Sequences

@slide build=all
Pre-sequences are turns or adjacency pairs that lead up to a particular (more important) adjacency pair

* For example, a question-answer pair which occurs before an invitation
  * Leads up to a particular ‘core’ adjacency pair
* Pre-invitation, pre-question, pre-request, and pre-announcements are most common

--

@transcript overlay
  a: Are you doing anything on Friday?
  b: No, why?
  a: We’re all going to the pub.
  b: That sounds nice.
  a: Would you like to come along?
  b: That sounds lovely.

--

@transcript overlay
  {answer ^^pre-invitation (=question)^^} :: a: Are you doing anything on Friday?
  {answer ^^answer^^} :: b: No, why?
  [{answer ^^pre-invitation (=statement)^^}]{red} :: a: We’re all going to the pub.
  [{answer ^^acknowledgement^^}]{red} :: b: That sounds nice.
  [{answer ^^invitation^^}]{gold} :: a: Would you like to come along?
  [{answer ^^acceptance^^}]{gold} :: b: That sounds lovely.

--

@transcript overlay
  a: Are you doing anything on Friday?
  b: No, why?
  a: We’re all going to the pub.
  b: That sounds nice.
  a: Would you like to come along?
  b: That sounds lovely.

--

@transcript overlay
  a: Are you doing anything on Friday?
  b: No, why?
  a: I just wondered. Popular people usually have plans on Fridays.

--

@transcript overlay
  a: Are you doing anything on Friday?
  b: Yes, I’m off to Paris for the weekend.
  a: Lucky you!

--

@transcript overlay
  a: Are you doing anything on Friday?
  b: No, why?
  a: Oh good, we’re looking for someone to babysit on Friday night.

--

@transcript overlay
  a: Are you doing anything on Friday?
  b: No, why?
  a: Oh good, we’re looking for someone to babysit on Friday night.
  b: Ah, I thought you meant Friday *daytime*.

@slide build=all
A conversation closes with:

* Closing signals (*Okay, All right, So, Right, Good, Well*)
* Terminal elements (*Bye, then, Bye, See you*)

@menu conversation 9
## Closings

@slide build=all
Pre-closings are the build-up to a conversation ending (often several moves):

@columns cols=2
* Future arrangements
* Broad indication of future meeting
* C/R makes excuses
* Onus shifted to C
* Onus shifted to R
* Reason for call
* Solicitudes
* Back references
* Thanking
@end

@menu conversation 9
## Closings

@slide build=all
Standard closings are four turns, disrupted by:

* Misplacement markers (*Oh, by the way*)
* Foreshortened closing (*Right, okay, bye then*)
* Extended closing (many closing signals, reluctance to start terminal elements)

@menu conversation 10 heading=build
## Errors

@slide build=all
Speakers can make mistakes and hearers can mishear:

@cards cols=2
### Speakers
* Hesitation phenomena (repetition, pauses, fillers, place-finders, changes)
* Speech errors (slips of the tongue, mispronunciation, malapropisms)
* Memory problem
* Interaction errors (overlaps, misplacement markers)
* Wrong information
* Insufficient information/ambiguity
::
### Hearers
* Speaker is unclear
* Background noise
* Hearer has hearing problem
* Hearer is a child, has medical issues, uncertain grasp of language
* Hearer mistakes the illocution
* Hearer has wrong world-knowledge
@end

@menu conversation 11 heading=build
## Repairs

@slide build=all
A repair resolves a communication problem (eg because of an error) by making a correction at a TRP:

* The speaker can notice their own error, or the hearer can
  * [Self-initiated or other-initiated]{italic}
* The speaker can correct their own error, or the hearer can
  * [Self-repair or other-repair]{italic}
* Obvious politeness preference for self-repair

--

## Repairs

@slide build=all
Where can a repair happen:

@cards cols=5 arrows build head=32 text=38
### Opportunity 1
self-initiated self-repair during the same turn as the error
::
### Opportunity 2
self initiates repair at TRP and self-repairs
::
### Opportunity 3
self initiates repair at TRP and other-repairs
::
### Opportunity 4
other initiates repair at TRP and self-repairs
::
### Opportunity 5
other initiates repair at TRP and other-repairs
@end
