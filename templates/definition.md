## Definition: Single Term

### Corpus Linguistics

**Definition:** The study of language through analysis of large, structured collections of authentic texts (corpora), using computational methods to identify patterns and frequencies.

**Etymology:** From Latin *corpus* ("body"), referring to a body of texts

**Key characteristics:**
* Empirical, data-driven approach
* Focus on authentic language use
* Quantitative and qualitative analysis
* Evidence-based claims about language patterns

### Phoneme

A phoneme is the smallest unit of sound in a language that can distinguish meaning between words.

**Examples in English:**
* /p/ vs. /b/: "pat" vs. "bat"
* /s/ vs. /z/: "sip" vs. "zip"
* /m/ vs. /n/: "sum" vs. "sun"

## Definition: Multiple Related Terms

### Key Concepts in Discourse Analysis

**Cohesion**
: Grammatical and lexical connections between elements in a text (reference, substitution, ellipsis, conjunction, lexical cohesion)

**Coherence**
: The semantic unity and logical connection of ideas that makes a text meaningful to readers/listeners

**Context**
: The situational, cultural, and linguistic environment in which communication occurs

**Register**
: Variety of language defined by its use in specific social situations (formal/informal, technical/general)

## Definition: Nested Conceptual Structure

### Semiotics (Parent Concept)

The general study of signs and symbols and their use or interpretation

<div style="margin-left: 2em;">

#### Semantics (Sub-concept 1)
The study of meaning in language: what words, phrases, and sentences mean

* **Lexical semantics:** Meaning of individual words
* **Compositional semantics:** How meanings combine
* **Pragmatic meaning:** Context-dependent interpretation

#### Syntax (Sub-concept 2)
The arrangement of words and phrases to create well-formed sentences

* **Phrase structure:** How words group into constituents
* **Dependencies:** Grammatical relationships between elements
* **Word order:** Sequence constraints and variations

#### Pragmatics (Sub-concept 3)
The study of how context influences meaning and use

* **Speech acts:** What we do with language
* **Implicature:** Implied rather than explicit meaning
* **Deixis:** Context-dependent reference

</div>

## Definition: Technical Term with Context

### Hapax Legomenon

**Literal meaning:** Greek, "said once"

**Technical definition:** A word or form that occurs only once within a specific context—either in an author's works, in a given corpus, or in an entire language's recorded history.

**Academic significance:**
* Challenges for lexicographers and translators
* Evidence for authorship attribution
* Indicators of linguistic creativity or error
* Statistical implications for corpus analysis

**Examples:**
* *Honorificabilitudinitatibus* (Shakespeare, *Love's Labour's Lost*)
* *Floccipaucinihilipilification* (rare but attested in English)

**Related concepts:** Dis legomenon (occurs twice), rare words, nonce formations

## Definition: Process-Based

### Operationalization (Research Methods)

**What it is:**
The process of defining a fuzzy, conceptual variable in precise, measurable terms

**Why it matters:**
Enables empirical research on abstract concepts by creating concrete, testable indicators

**How it works:**

1. **Identify conceptual variable** (e.g., "social cohesion")
2. **Specify dimensions** (e.g., trust, shared values, networks)
3. **Select indicators** (e.g., survey items, network density measures)
4. **Define measurement** (e.g., 5-point Likert scale, statistical metrics)
5. **Establish validity** (does this truly measure the concept?)

**Example:** "Intelligence" → IQ test scores, academic achievement, problem-solving speed

## Definition: Contrasting Definitions

### Two Schools of Thought on "Culture"

<div class="container">
<div class="col">

#### Anthropological View
(Tylor, 1871; Geertz, 1973)

"Culture is that complex whole which includes knowledge, belief, art, morals, law, custom, and any other capabilities and habits acquired by man as a member of society."

**Focus:** Holistic, symbolic systems, shared meanings

</div>
<div class="col">

#### Sociological View
(Bourdieu, 1984; Williams, 1983)

"Culture consists of socially produced and shared patterns of thought, behavior, and material products that reflect and reproduce power relations."

**Focus:** Social structures, cultural capital, ideology

</div>
</div>

**Synthesis:** Contemporary approaches often integrate both perspectives, examining symbolic meanings within structural contexts
