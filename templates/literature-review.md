## Literature Review: Single Source Deep-Dive

### Seminal Work: Chomsky (1957) *Syntactic Structures*

**Historical Context**
* Published during behaviorist dominance in psychology
* Challenged Skinner's verbal behavior model
* Introduced formal mathematical approach to linguistics

**Key Arguments**
1. Language is governed by abstract syntactic rules
2. Phrase structure grammars inadequate; transformational rules needed
3. Linguistic competence vs. performance distinction
4. Innate language acquisition device hypothesis

**Impact & Reception**
* Initially controversial; gradually paradigm-shifting
* Founded generative grammar research program
* Influenced cognitive science, computer science, philosophy
* Critiqued by functionalists, usage-based theorists

**Contemporary Relevance**
* Minimalist Program extends core insights
* Debates continue: universal grammar vs. emergentism
* Computational linguistics builds on formal frameworks

**How This Informs My Research:** Provides theoretical foundation for syntactic analysis, though my approach incorporates corpus data alongside intuitions

## Literature Review: Chronological Development

### The Evolution of Corpus Linguistics: Three Waves

**First Wave: 1960s-1980s — Foundational**

* **Key figures:** Quirk, Svartvik, Francis, Kučera
* **Focus:** Building first major corpora (Brown, LOB, London-Lund)
* **Methods:** Concordancing, frequency analysis
* **Contributions:** Demonstrated value of empirical data; established corpus compilation principles

**Second Wave: 1990s-2000s — Expansion**

* **Key figures:** Biber, Sinclair, Hunston, Stubbs
* **Focus:** Genre analysis, phraseology, register variation
* **Methods:** Multi-dimensional analysis, collocation studies
* **Contributions:** Sophisticated analytical frameworks; corpus-driven vs. corpus-based distinction

**Third Wave: 2010s-present — Integration**

* **Key figures:** Gries, Brezina, Egbert, Baker
* **Focus:** Mixed methods, critical approaches, learner corpora
* **Methods:** Statistics, visualization, qualitative integration
* **Contributions:** Methodological rigor; social/political awareness; computational advances

**Current Trends:** Large-scale web corpora, real-time monitoring, multimodal corpora, corpus-assisted discourse studies

## Literature Review: Gap Analysis

### What We Know vs. What We Need to Know

**Well-Established Findings:**

✓ Bilingual advantages in executive function (Bialystok et al., 2012; Baum & Titone, 2014)
✓ Language switching recruits cognitive control networks (Abutalebi & Green, 2016)
✓ Earlier bilingualism → stronger effects (DeLuca et al., 2019)

**Debated/Inconsistent Findings:**

⚠ Magnitude of bilingual advantage (meta-analyses show publication bias)
⚠ Whether advantage extends beyond specific tasks to general intelligence
⚠ Socioeconomic confounds in many studies (bilinguals ≠ monolinguals in SES)

**Significant Gaps:**

❌ Mechanisms: *how* does bilingual experience reshape brain function?
❌ Individual differences: *who* shows advantages and why?
❌ Lifespan trajectory: developmental and aging processes
❌ Ecological validity: lab tasks vs. real-world cognitive demands

**My Contribution:** Longitudinal neuroimaging study tracking bilingual and monolingual children over 3 years, controlling for SES, measuring real-world executive function outcomes

## Literature Review: Theoretical Debate

### Competing Frameworks for Understanding Language Change

<div class="container">
<div class="col">

#### Formalist Position

**Representatives:** Lightfoot, Roberts, Kroch

**Core claim:** Grammar change results from child language acquisition re-analyzing input based on innate UG constraints

**Evidence:**
* Parametric changes (e.g., V2 → SVO)
* Rapid, discrete shifts
* Cross-linguistic patterns

**Critique:** Underestimates social factors, usage effects

</div>
<div class="col">

#### Functionalist Position

**Representatives:** Hopper, Traugott, Bybee

**Core claim:** Language change driven by communicative needs, frequency effects, processing pressures

**Evidence:**
* Grammaticalization pathways
* Gradient, usage-based changes
* Pragmatic motivations

**Critique:** Struggles to explain constraints on possible changes

</div>
</div>

**Attempted Syntheses:**
* Croft (2000): Evolutionary framework
* Heine & Kuteva (2007): Grammaticalization as universal tendency within constraints
* Bybee (2010): Usage-based approach acknowledging cognitive constraints

**Where I Stand:** Change involves both cognitive constraints and social/functional pressures; need multi-level models

## Literature Review: Meta-Analysis Summary

### Effects of Feedback on Second Language Writing: Meta-Analysis

**Research Question:** Does written corrective feedback improve L2 writing accuracy?

**Scope:**
* 41 studies, 1989-2018
* Total N = 2,947 learners
* 15 languages, diverse proficiency levels

**Overall Finding:**
* **Medium effect size** (Cohen's d = 0.61, 95% CI [0.48, 0.74])
* **Statistically significant** across studies
* **But high heterogeneity** (I² = 68%)

**Moderator Analysis:**

| Moderator | Effect | Interpretation |
|-----------|--------|----------------|
| **Feedback Type** | Direct > Indirect (d = 0.72 vs. 0.48) | Explicit correction more effective |
| **Linguistic Target** | Morphology > Syntax (d = 0.69 vs. 0.51) | Rule-based features more amenable |
| **Timing** | Immediate > Delayed (d = 0.67 vs. 0.54) | Temporal proximity matters |
| **Revision** | With revision > No revision (d = 0.79 vs. 0.42) | Active processing crucial |

**Conclusion:** Feedback works, but effectiveness depends on type, target, implementation.

**Implications for Practice:** Prioritize direct feedback with revision opportunities, especially for morphological features

## Literature Review: Systematic Review Structure

### Mobile-Assisted Language Learning (MALL): Systematic Review

**Selection Process**
* Initial search: 1,243 articles (2010-2023)
* After screening: 87 empirical studies included
* Databases: ERIC, LLBA, Web of Science

**Study Characteristics**
* 65% quantitative, 20% qualitative, 15% mixed methods
* Most common languages: English (58%), Spanish (12%), Chinese (10%)
* Contexts: Higher ed (47%), K-12 (28%), Adult ed (15%), Self-directed (10%)

**Findings by Research Question:**

*RQ1: What learning outcomes are targeted?*
* Vocabulary: 52 studies (most common)
* Speaking/pronunciation: 18 studies
* Grammar: 12 studies
* Reading: 8 studies
* Writing: 5 studies

*RQ2: What technologies are used?*
* Custom apps: 41 studies
* SMS/messaging: 23 studies
* Commercial apps (Duolingo, etc.): 15 studies
* AR/VR: 8 studies

*RQ3: What are the reported outcomes?*
* Positive effects: 81% of studies
* Null findings: 15% of studies
* Negative effects: 4% of studies
* **But:** Publication bias likely; small sample sizes common (median N = 45)

**Identified Gaps:**
* Limited long-term follow-up
* Lack of theory-driven research
* Minimal attention to individual differences
* Need for cost-benefit analyses

## Literature Review: Annotated Key Sources

### Essential Readings on Critical Discourse Analysis

**Fairclough, N. (1995). *Critical Discourse Analysis: The Critical Study of Language.***
* Foundational text establishing CDA as interdisciplinary field
* Three-dimensional framework: text, discursive practice, social practice
* Emphasis on power relations and ideology in language
* Use for: Theoretical grounding, methodological framework

**van Dijk, T. A. (2008). *Discourse and Power.***
* Socio-cognitive approach to CDA
* Focus on knowledge, ideologies, context models
* Detailed analysis of news discourse, racism, political discourse
* Use for: Cognitive dimensions of discourse, media analysis

**Wodak, R. & Meyer, M. (Eds.) (2016). *Methods of Critical Discourse Studies.***
* Comprehensive overview of CDA methodologies
* Discourse-Historical Approach, Social Actors approach, Multimodality
* Practical analytical procedures with examples
* Use for: Methodological guidance, comparison of approaches

**Baker, P. et al. (2008). "A useful methodological synergy? Combining critical discourse analysis and corpus linguistics."**
* Argues for integration of corpus methods with CDA
* Addresses critique that CDA cherry-picks examples
* Demonstrates complementarity: corpus identifies patterns, CDA interprets
* Use for: Justifying mixed-method approach in my research
