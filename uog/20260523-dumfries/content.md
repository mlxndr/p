## Award of Credit Reforms

**Three interdependent elements:**

@cards style=header
:: light-gold
### Credit based on achievement

Credit awarded for courses with a satisfactory grade (D3 or above)

Degree awards are based only on course grades and not programme-wide calculations
:: light-gold
### Compensatory credit at session level

Students who do not pass all courses in a session but have ≥ D3 in two-thirds or more of that session’s credits have shown engagement and are awarded credit for all courses in the session

Clear, automatic support for students with EC or small volumes of unremedied work
:: light-gold
### Honours rules aligned with all other levels

Limited reassessment introduced at Honours where not already present, as already exists at every other level

Resit grades fully capped at C3/D3; only first-diet grades count for classification
@end

@rule

1\. This only changes the award of credit (“should the grades count towards a degree award?”) and *does not* affect either the grades awarded or the GPA thresholds for award of a degree.

2\. These elements work together. Credit based on achievement requires a route to remedy failure; that route requires a compensation mechanism for circumstances where remedy is not possible.

## AoC: Summary of Changes

@cards style=header
:: orange
### What changes

How we count credit: course-by-course and session-level and with session GPAs, not programme-wide percentages or calculations

All “75% rules” are removed

Honours comes into line with all other levels (including rules on reassessment)

Reassessment grades capped at D3/C3 (currently only grade points are capped)
:: orange
### What does not change

Classification thresholds and GPA requirements for awards

The grades required for degree classification

Any course/degree requirements which change the award of credit (e.g. PSRB requirements in Medicine, Veterinary Medicine, Engineering, Education, Nursing, etc.)

Only first-diet grades used for classification
@end

@rule

Remember that a student can still graduate with a failed course on their transcript – as they can now. The failed grade counts in their GPA, just as it does now. We are not lowering the bar for degree awards: we are simplifying how we calculate the volume of work that goes into a degree calculation.

## Reassessment Changes

**What’s new:** Limited reassessment at Honours (where it didn't previously exist). This brings Honours into line with every other level of study. Resit grades fully capped at D3 (UG) and C3 (PGT) – grades *and* grade points.

**What’s the same:** Only first-diet grades count for classification. A resit can lift a student over a progression or award-of-degree threshold, but cannot lift them into a higher classification.

**What’s changed:** *Only* students who need reassessment for PSRB requirements or to hit a progression GPA or to graduate are given reassessment opportunities. (Not everyone who failed a course.) Students who already have enough credit through compensation do not sit again. Far fewer resits for staff and students – even with Honours reassessment options added in.

## Award of Credit Benefits

**One set of Code of Assessment credit rules** across all levels: far easier to remember, easier to advise students, easier to move between mindset of Honours and pre-Honours and Masters

**No more programme-wide percentage calculations of submission** figures per student across years of assessment; vastly simpler calculations and easier to check

**Net reduction in marking workload** (even with Honours reassessment): reassessment eligibility is limited only to students who need reassessment to hit a progression GPA or to graduate, and the first-diet rule, compensatory credit, and capped reassessment grades remove pressure for unnecessary resits (e.g. at Masters level)

Maintains high academic standards: **credit now requires achievement**

## DR: SES/CoSS UG

For undergraduate degrees the new consolidated regulations preserve almost all existing provisions. The main changes are structural:

* Progression standardised (9.0 for UG)
* Removing otiose requirements like credit-count progress thresholds
* Some programme-specific detail (including what used to be called Qualifying Courses) moves from generic regulations to programme specifications, which Boards of Study control directly
* Progress Committees renamed Academic Review Panels (same function)

One substantive change: Honours classification weightings are being rationalised across the university to two permitted options (50:50 and 25:75).

## Beyond Regs: Moodle/Handbooks

New **governance and standardisation of student-facing local content** (student ‘handbooks’): All student-facing content must not be repeated or glossed, and now has sign-off requirements and lives in one of four tiers:

  * *Institutional:* written centrally, owned by Registry/ASG
  * *College:* specific to College-level arrangements
  * *School:* School-level practices and procedures
  * *Programme or course:* the local material you actually own

**Standardisation of Moodle** use

  * one course code = one Moodle course
  * all assessment outcomes stored in Gradebook, no MyGrades opt-outs
  * standardised naming conventions for assessments, etc.

## [[Delayed Assessment Permits]]{light-purple}

**These sit alongside ECs, not instead of them.**

* Three permits per academic year, on top of the full EC system, instead of extensions
* Each gives a five-day extension on one piece of coursework
* (Not exams – but a discussion to happen this year)

**How it works:**

* Students notify via the online system before the deadline
* Extension applies automatically
* After three are used, appointment arranged with SSO

**When to use them:** Short-term disruption where students shouldn’t have evidence and don’t need wider support

---

@slide placeholder text=24

## [[DAPs and ECs]]{light-purple}

**DAPs are for:** A bad week, a minor illness, juggling life stresses – wider than the previous extensions rationale (*de jure…*).

**ECs are for:** Serious circumstances where students have evidence and may need support.

When UCL brought in in this system, three permits was more than enough for 76% of students. The rest had serious circumstances – and the full EC system was there for them.

---

@slide placeholder text=30

## [[Cancel-and-Resit]]{light-gold}

(Name still under discussion!)

**Current system:**

* Student sits an exam while unwell → claim “affected performance” before grades are released → approved at Stage 1 → wait months for a Board decision while being uncertain about the summer resits and your plans

**How the new system works:**

* Sit an exam while unwell/etc
* Claim with evidence before grades are released that you shouldn’t have sat the exam
* After stage 1, your attempt is immediately deleted
* You get a fresh attempt at the next opportunity, no penalty
* Decision is immediate after the evidence check

---

@slide placeholder text=30

## [[Gradated Evidence]]{light-blue}

**Current approach:**

* Every EC claim requires the same level of evidence, regardless of history
* Students with very serious and upsetting circumstances asked to get evidence

**Proposal:**

* First claim in a session: self-declaration for defined circumstances (bereavement, pregnancy loss, etc.)
* Automatic referral to wellbeing support
* Evidence requirements increase only with repeated claims
